# Ammo Counting

A Ruby on Rails **game** about a diverse array of ammunition calibers and their
prices. Ammunition supply and pricing is critically important to personal and national
security. Your job as the player is to maximize your portfolio value over time.

Each user has an account and their own ammo portfolio. Every new user starts
with **$1,776 USD** to spend on ammunition, on 2026-01-01 of the game calendar.
Prices for each caliber fluctuate over time (simulated), and those fluctuations
determine whether a user's net worth goes up or down. The UI shows each
logged-in user their historical portfolio value.

### The game clock

Time is **turn-based and per player**. Your game date moves only when you
**fast-forward** (+1 day, week, month, 3 months or 1 year, never more than a year
at once, and never backwards), simulating a long wait before your next buy/sell
decision. You cannot trade while time passes: the price path you skipped is revalued
into your books day by day, so your statements and net worth chart cover it. The game ends 128
years after the start. Everything you see and do is dated by your own game
clock (never the wall clock), and other players' clocks do not affect yours.

Every player lives in the **same price world**: one deterministic, seeded price
history, generated on demand (one tick per game day) only as far as the furthest
player has reached. You never see prices later than your game date.

### Market events

Every 6 to 10 years, a crisis spikes prices for a readable reason ("The Bubonic
Plague is back!", "Democrats have been elected to a federal trifecta", "Global
conflicts have driven a spike in military demand for ammo", ...). Prices climb for
weeks, then the crisis ends, prices unwind, and the normal random walk continues.
The dashboard shows the news, and fast-forwarding past an event tells you what
happened. The schedule is a pure function of the price seed (`MarketEvent`).

## Design principles

**Simplicity and correctness**, in that order of tiebreak: when a feature
threatens correctness, drop the feature.

- Money is stored as **integer cents**. No floating point for money anywhere.
- Prices are **integer centicents (1/100 cent) per round**, so cheap rounds can
  move by realistic amounts; a position's value is rounded half-up to whole
  cents. Quantities are whole rounds.
- Rails never does arithmetic on money, prices or quantities; the SPARK engine
  does all of it (proved at GNATprove level 4). Rails stores and retrieves integers.
  An AST-based ratchet test (`test/architecture/no_money_math_test.rb`) fails on
  arithmetic in the app, and every exception is a justified entry in
  `test/architecture/math_allowlist.yml`.

## Architecture

```
 browser ──▶ Rails app ──▶ ledger_core (SPARK Ada)   all balances & postings
                 │
                 └──────▶ mock price index           caliber prices over time
```

### Rails app (repo root)

Accounts and authentication, calibers, portfolio and history views. Uses
SQLite, Hotwire (Turbo/Stimulus) and importmap, as generated by Rails 8.1. The
database stores *display data and a mirror of ledger results*; the ledger is the
authority for balances.

### `ledger_core/` — accounting engine (SPARK Ada)

A double-entry ledger written in SPARK so that GNATprove can mathematically
verify its key properties:

- **Debits always equal credits** in every posted transaction, and therefore
  across the whole ledger.
- No arithmetic overflow, no out-of-range values, no runtime errors.
- A user's cash cannot go negative: a purchase that exceeds cash is rejected.

Accounts per user are roughly: `Cash` (USD), `Holdings:<caliber>` (rounds, valued
at the current price), and a counterparty/`Market` account that is the other side
of every buy and sell. Revaluation (price moves) posts to an unrealized gain/loss
account so the books still balance.

Built with [Alire](https://alire.ada.dev/):

```sh
cd ledger_core
alr build            # compile
alr exec -- gnatprove -P ledger_core.gpr --level=4   # prove (GNATprove comes via Alire)
(cd tests && alr build && ./bin/tests)                # unit tests
LEDGER_CORE_LIBRARY_TYPE=relocatable alr build        # shared lib for Rails (scripts/build.sh does this)
```

Rails calls the engine in-process through a C-ABI shared library using Ruby
`ffi` (see `ledger_core/ABI.md`). The default path is
`ledger_core/lib/libLedger_Core.so`; set `LEDGER_CORE_LIB` to load it from
elsewhere (the Docker image does). The library needs the Ada runtime
(`libgnat`) next to it or on the loader path; see
[docs/DEPLOYMENT.md](docs/DEPLOYMENT.md). Rails persists postings in SQLite; the
engine validates each transaction before it is committed.

### Financial statements

On demand, for any user, the app produces the three core financial statements
from that user's ledger. All figures come from the ledger engine, never from
separate Rails-side arithmetic, so the statements always agree with the books.

- **Balance sheet** — as of a point in time: assets (cash, holdings at market
  value), liabilities, and equity. Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
- **Income statement** — for a period: realized and unrealized gains/losses
  from ammo trades and price moves, net income for the period.
- **Statement of cash flows** — for a period: opening cash, cash in/out from
  buying and selling ammo and from the initial $1,776 funding, closing cash.

Required consistency (proved in the engine where possible, otherwise covered by
the unit and differential tests; see TODO.md for the proof status):

- The balance sheet balances at every point in time.
- Net income on the income statement equals the change in retained earnings.
- Closing cash on the cash flow statement equals cash on the balance sheet at
  the period end; opening cash equals cash at the period start.
- The initial funding is owner contributed capital (equity), not income.

### Mock price index

A simulated price service the app calls to get the current price of each
caliber. It is a stand-in for a real market-data feed, so the Rails app talks to
it through a narrow in-process Ruby interface (`current_prices` → list of `{caliber, price_cents,
as_of}`) and could later swap in a real source. Prices follow a seeded random
walk so runs are reproducible and tests are deterministic. A scheduled Solid
Timeline service extends the shared price history, one tick per game day, as
players fast-forward (there is no scheduled tick job), and every user shares one
price history.

### Portfolio history charts

The portfolio history chart supports the ranges **1D, 1W, 1M, YTD, 1Y and MAX**.
History is kept indefinitely (the design ceiling is 128 years, roughly a human
lifespan), so older data is rolled up into coarser buckets rather than deleted.

### Trading and valuation

Users buy and sell whole rounds at the current price plus a spread and fee (no
shorting, no borrowing; cash and holdings never go negative). Holdings are
marked to market on every tick, with unrealized gain/loss posted to the ledger,
and cost basis is tracked per holding so sales produce realized gains. Each
user chooses at runtime whether cost basis is shown as **weighted average** or
**FIFO**; the choice applies to the portfolio view and the financial statements.
Net worth and cash don't depend on the choice; only the split between realized
and unrealized gain does.

## Requirements

- Ruby 3.4.11 (see `.ruby-version`), Bundler
- SQLite 3
- Alire and a GNAT toolchain (for `ledger_core`); GNATprove for verification

## Setup, run, test

```sh
scripts/build.sh   # install gems, build ledger_core
scripts/run.sh     # build, prepare DB, start the server
scripts/test.sh    # every mandatory check (what CI runs)
```

Quality and security gates (brakeman, bundler-audit, rubocop, 100% test
coverage, SPARK proofs) are mandatory. See [STANDARDS.md](STANDARDS.md).

## Deployment

The `Dockerfile` installs Alire and GNAT in a build stage, builds the
relocatable `ledger_core` library, ships it with its Ada runtime
(`libgnat`, via an `$ORIGIN` RPATH) and sets `LEDGER_CORE_LIB`. The image is
x86_64 only. The Kamal config (`config/deploy.yml`) still has the generated
placeholder name, registry and host.

All state is SQLite files on the `ammocounting_storage` volume. A volume is
not a backup, and the files must not be copied while the app runs (WAL mode);
take snapshots with `bin/kamal backup` and copy them off the server.

Details, backup and restore: [docs/DEPLOYMENT.md](docs/DEPLOYMENT.md).
